    Choosing a registration method¶

    The right registration method depends far more on how your data were acquired than on any parameter. This page helps you pick a path, shows what each path achieved on real data, and explains how to read the validation numbers SMINT reports, including two cases where a good-looking number means nothing.

    Decision tree¶

    flowchart TD
    A{"Was the second modality measured on the<br/><b>same physical section</b> as the ST data?"}
    A -- "yes<br/>(post-stain nuclei on the Xenium section)" --> B{"Both centroid sets in µm, and<br/>within ~max_distance (100 µm)?"}
    B -- no --> B1["Convert pixels to µm and<br/>pre-register first<br/><code>smint-alignment pretransform</code>"]
    B1 --> C
    B -- yes --> C["<b>multiround</b> (default)<br/>displacement voting: affine,<br/>then smooth TPS if it helps"]
    C --> D{"Need every nucleus<br/>on exactly one cell?"}
    D -- "yes (default)" --> E["Snap: <code>refine_by_assignment</code><br/>keep status = snapped<br/>for verified pairs only"]
    D -- "no, smooth geometry only" --> F["<code>--no-refine</code> /<br/><code>refine=False</code>"]

    A -- "no<br/>(MALDI or ST on a neighbouring section)" --> G["Pre-register: flip → rotate → scale → translate<br/>napari <i>2. Pre-register</i> or <code>pretransform --search</code>"]
    G --> H["Pick paired landmarks<br/>same features, same order, ≥ 3"]
    H --> I["<b>STalign LDDMM</b><br/>orientation = stalign"]

    The first question is the important one. On the same section each nucleus really has a partner Xenium cell, so SMINT solves a matching problem and can place nuclei exactly. On sequential sections no cell has a partner, so SMINT solves an image-warping problem. Running the same-section tools on sequential sections still produces a number, but it is fitting noise: the "partners" it finds are merely nearby cells.

    Same section: which centroid method¶

    register_centroids (and smint-alignment centroid --method) offers five models. Use multiround unless you have a reason not to.

    Method What it fits When it works Watch out for
    multiround (default) Round 1: affine through per-block displacement peaks (500 µm blocks). Round 2: smooth TPS through finer peaks (250 µm), kept only if it helps Starting offset comparable to or larger than the cell spacing; local warps Tissue that is a regular lattice, where every whole-spacing shift looks equally good
    affine Least-squares affine on nearest-neighbour pairs Offset well below the cell spacing, purely global Wrong pairs bias the fit
    ransac RANSAC-robust affine on nearest-neighbour pairs As affine, with some bad pairs Cannot recover when most pairs are wrong
    tps Affine, then thin-plate spline on the pairs Small offset with local distortion Memorises pairs when under-regularised
    ransac+tps RANSAC affine, then TPS on the inliers As tps, with outliers Same as tps

    Why multiround. The single-round methods match every nucleus to its nearest cell once, before fitting. When the starting offset is close to the cell spacing, many of those first matches are wrong, and no transform fitted to them can recover. multiround never commits to matches: within each block, every nucleus votes for the displacements to several nearby cells. Wrong candidates scatter, but the true offset is shared by the whole block and piles up as a peak.

    When to snap. A smooth transform leaves each nucleus a few microns from its cell, partly because a nucleus really is off-centre in its cell. refine_by_assignment pairs nuclei and cells one-to-one, keeps a pair only if its offset agrees with its neighbours' offsets, and moves the nucleus exactly onto the cell (snapped). Nuclei that fail the neighbourhood check can then be assigned to the nearest free cell (assigned); these are less certain. It runs by default in register_centroid_files and the CLI.

    Sequential sections: pre-register first¶

    STalign's LDDMM refines an alignment; it does not find one from scratch. Spatial metabolomics usually arrives on its own pixel grid, often rotated or mirrored and at a different scale, so bring it roughly onto the ST data first (see Coordinate frames). Then place at least three landmark pairs, the same anatomical features in the same order in both datasets, and run LDDMM. Correspondence is by position in the list, so a swapped pair silently produces a wrong warp.

    Measured on real data¶

    88,906 post-stain nuclei registered onto 74,247 Xenium cells, whose centroids are about 17 µm apart. Numbers are for the quarter of nuclei held out from fitting.

    Held-out nuclei Median distance to nearest cell Mutual match ≤ 5 µm Mutual match ≤ 10 µm
    No transform 12.4 µm 10% 34%
    ransac+tps (one round) 11.5 µm 13% 40%
    multiround 5.4 µm 45% 76%

    Held-out TRE fell from 11.0 to 4.9 µm, in 3 s. Snapping then placed 65,349 nuclei, 88% of the Xenium cells, exactly on a distinct cell: 57,083 snapped and 8,266 assigned. The median neighbour deviation was 4.1 µm for snapped pairs and 8.5 µm for assigned ones, so treat the assigned pairs with more caution.

    459,969 MALDI pixels registered onto 53,622 Xenium cells of the neighbouring section with 11 landmark pairs, in about 72 s on one A30 GPU. After LDDMM the median distance from an SM pixel to the nearest ST cell was 16.4 µm.

    There is no ground truth on sequential sections, so this distance is a sanity check (the pixels landed on tissue), not an accuracy. Judge the result by overlaying anatomical features you did not use as landmarks.

    Reading the validation numbers¶

    Same-section metrics¶

    Key in the result Meaning Trust it?
    tre_initial Mean distance between matched pairs before any transform A baseline
    tre_fit The same after the transform, on the pairs used to fit it No: see trap 1
    tre_validation The same on held-out data (pairs, or for multiround held-out nuclei matched by mutual nearest neighbour) Yes, with trap 1 in mind
    validation["before"], ["after"] (multiround) Correspondence-free: median_nn and the fractions mutual_within_5 / mutual_within_10 for held-out nuclei Yes
    rounds (multiround) Per-round log: blocks, consistent fraction, median NN, whether the round was kept For diagnosis
    refinement["validation"] Held-out check of snapping: kept pairs predicted from their neighbours alone Yes: see trap 2
    registration_status, neighbour_deviation (per row) How each nucleus was placed, and how far its offset is from its neighbourhood's Filter on these

    SMINT logs a warning when held-out TRE is no better than the initial TRE, when validation TRE exceeds five times fit TRE (single-round methods), and when assigned pairs deviate far more than snapped ones. Read the log.

    Trap 1: TRE against nearest-neighbour pairs is circular¶

    Correspondences are found by nearest neighbour, so TRE measures distance to the nearest cell, not to the right one. A flexible enough transform drives it to zero on the pairs it was fitted to: that is interpolation, not accuracy. Holding pairs out protects you against over-fitting, but not against the pairs themselves being wrong.

    How to protect yourself:

    • Compare TRE with the cell spacing. If the initial TRE is a large fraction of the spacing, nearest-neighbour pairs are ambiguous and the single-round methods cannot be trusted. Use multiround.
    • Prefer the correspondence-free numbers (validation["after"]) and an overlay plot.
    • Never set validation_fraction=0 unless you accept that the reported TRE is then uninformative.

    Trap 2: a snapped nucleus has zero residual by construction¶

    Snapping moves a nucleus exactly onto its partner cell, so its residual is 0 whether the partner is right or wrong. The meaningful check is refinement["validation"]: a tenth of the accepted pairs are left out of the offset field and predicted from their neighbours alone. On Sample 2 z5 the median neighbour deviation was 4.1 µm for snapped and 8.5 µm for assigned pairs; a nucleus put on a neighbour's cell deviates by about a cell spacing.

    Keep only verified pairs

    For analyses where a wrong nucleus–cell pair would mislead you, keep rows with registration_status == "snapped", filter on neighbour_deviation, or run with --no-assign-remaining (assign_remaining=False).
